What is road efficiency?

An efficient layout connects every building that needs a road to the Town Hall using as few road tiles as possible.

Why is it important?

Every road tile you reclaim leaves more room for buildings that earn Forge Points, produce goods, or boost your army.

How is it calculated?

FoE Helper and Forge Hammer measure road efficiency by how many road tiles you actually use with how many they estimate your buildings need.

Road efficiency = (baseline road tiles ÷ actual road tiles) × 100%

For each building that needs a road, calculate shorter side × required road width (1 or 2) ÷ 2, with both lengths measured in tiles. Add the results to get your city’s baseline road tile count.

Picture a street lined on both sides with buildings, each with its short side facing the road. The formula divides by two because the buildings across the street share the same pavement. Connect those buildings with fewer tiles than the baseline and your score goes above 100%.

A city at 135% efficiency

The city in the screenshot has a 54-tile baseline and uses 40 road tiles. That gives 54 ÷ 40 × 100% = 135% efficiency. The layout saves 14 tiles compared with the baseline, just two tiles shy of a full expansion.

We could swap the Weeping Castle and the Boo’loon Grand Gala to save one more tile and reach about 138.5%. but it just doesn’t look right… So it’s up to you to decide how much of your city’s charm are you willing to sacrifice for a few extra percentage points.:)

General rules for better roads

  • Give the Town Hall room on all four sides. Set it back from the city edge so roads can reach it from any direction.
  • Fill both sides before extending a street. Place buildings that need road access along it, with their short sides facing the road where possible.
  • Move road-free buildings to the edges or tuck them behind the roadside row.
  • Put larger buildings at road ends. If a large building needs a connection, the road can stop as soon as it reaches the building’s edge.

Advanced layout tips

  1. Limit two-lane buildings. From the Progressive Era onward, look for production buildings from Quantum Incursions (QI) or events that meet your needs with a single-lane road or no road at all.
  2. Make the last tile serve three buildings. At the end of a one-tile-wide road, one building can touch the tip and one can sit on each side.
  3. Try a zigzag for small buildings. If you have a cluster of small buildings that all need road access, try arranging them around the bends of a zigzag street. See the forum example.
